DeepFire-Forecaster

      Under review
    

    
BurnBench/Jupyter + PyTorch/156 commits/MIT


    

      NOTICE
      Manuscript under review at MDPI Sensors. Not accepted, not published, no DOI. Results below are from the repository.
    


    
Wildfire remote sensing on the TS-SatFire benchmark (2025), which consolidates multispectral VIIRS image stacks from Suomi-NPP and NOAA-20 across three tasks: active fire detection, burned area mapping, and fire progression prediction.


    

      

        
Architecture — SpaSE-UNet3D

        
    
          
  • Spatial squeeze-and-excitation 3D U-Net, encoder channels [64, 128, 256, 512], ~32.9M parameters
    • 
          
  • ResBlock3D with spatial-only (1,3,3) convolutions — no temporal mixing across days
    • 
          
  • Squeeze-and-excitation channel attention at every encoder stage
    • 
          
  • ASPP bottleneck, dilation rates [1, 6, 12]
    • 
          
  • Deep supervision on decoder outputs; combined Dice + Focal loss
    • 
          
  • OneCycleLR, 80–100 epochs
    • 
          
  • One config switch (TS_LENGTH = 1 or 2) for a clean ablation
    • 
        

      

      

        
What the project contributes

        
    
          
  1. SpaSE-UNet3D. A spatial squeeze-and-excitation 3D U-Net built for the shape of this data. Spatial-only (1, 3, 3) convolutions refuse to mix across time, because the observation windows are too short for temporal mixing to pay for itself; squeeze-and-excitation attention reweights the VIIRS spectral bands dynamically instead. It reaches F1 0.8549 ± 0.0005 on active fire and 0.3845 ± 0.0221 on fire progression — matching or exceeding the strongest published baselines on their respective terms, using one third of their temporal context at 2.72× fewer parameters.
    2. 
          
  2. Label-quality audit. Many fires lack ground-truth annotations entirely. 18 training fires and 2 test fires are excluded for active fire — and the two unannotated test fires cannot be scored by any model, not just this one. Training on audited data raised active fire recall from ~0.80 to 0.86. The benchmark's own scoring procedure is documented too: it differs from ours in ways that make the two sets of figures incomparable.
    3. 
        

      

    


    
Results

    

      

        F1 micro-averaged over all test pixels, mean ± std. Test set of 15 verified fires, optimal inference threshold 0.20–0.22, active fire precision 0.848 and recall 0.861. IoU is derived from micro-F1 as F1/(2−F1). No baseline delta is quoted: the benchmark scores differently than we do, in ways that make the two sets of figures incomparable.

NEOW / neo_tracker

    
three.js/single HTML file/no build step


    
      
      
      
      
        ▸ Launch tracker
        Live orbital viewer · runs in this browser
      
    

    

      

        
Heliocentric solar system viewer built on NASA's NeoWs near-Earth object feed. It propagates eight planets, fifteen moons, and every tracked NEO from published osculating elements through a Kepler solver.

        
Publishes its own scale factors rather than faking scale, shows JPL orbit uncertainty codes, and explicitly corrects the "potentially hazardous means close now" misconception. Bilingual English / Ελληνικά. No dependencies beyond three.js.

      

      

        
Checked against independent ground truth

        
    
          
  • Earth perihelion 0.98329 AU, aphelion 1.01671 AU
    • 
          
  • Equinox and solstice solar longitudes within 0.4°
    • 
          
  • Kepler solver converges to machine precision through e = 0.99
    • 
          
  • Earth computed twice by unrelated routes, agreeing to 44,000 km and 59 arcseconds
    • 
        

        

          ▸Open tracker
          ▸Source
        

      

    

  


  

    
Sector 07 — Grid Expansion

    
three.js/WebGL2/Web Audio/single HTML file/no build step


    
      
      
      
      
        ▸ Enter the grid
        Isometric defence grid · runs in this browser
      
    

    

      

        
Isometric cyberpunk city-builder and defence game. You hold one megacity sector: build nodes onto empty pads, let them link into a network, and purge intrusions before their timers run out — six rings deep around a central spire.

        
The whole thing is one HTML file. Geometry, icons and audio are generated at runtime rather than loaded, and every balance number — costs, yields, steal rates, drone stats — sits in a single object at the top of the file.

      

      

        
What is actually in there

        
    
          
  • 117 plots across six rings, claimed outward from the core
    • 
          
  • Eight node classes over five tiers; each link pays +8%, an isolated node loses 25%
    • 
          
  • Six breach classes unlock as threat climbs SECURE → OVERRUN
    • 
          
  • Every building merges to ~3 draw calls, which is what makes tier-5 towers affordable
    • 
          
  • 1,400 citizens, 260 vehicles and ~1,100 data packets drawn as points
    • 
          
  • Audio is synthesised through the Web Audio API — no sound files
    •

          ▸Source

NCF Reproduction

      
PyTorch/ECE480

      
Reproduction of Neural Collaborative Filtering (He et al., WWW 2017) on MovieLens-100K. Every experiment repeated ten times with independent seeds, all results reported as mean ± standard deviation. MLP depth and GMF+MLP pretraining show no mean benefit at this dataset size, contradicting the paper's MovieLens-1M finding.

      
      

        NeuMF best HR@10 0.649 ± 0.011
        KD student HR@10 0.655 ± 0.010
        FitNets-style 0.582
        Open repository
